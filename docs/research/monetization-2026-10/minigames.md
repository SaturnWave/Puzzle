# Mini-games, bonus levels and alternate modes in top casual puzzle games

Research for the museum match-3 (Unity, 20 rooms, 1000 levels, jigsaw pieces of public-domain paintings).
Written 2026-10-23. **Every source in this file was opened or seen on 2026-10-23**; the full URL list is at the end (S1 to S61).

**Labels on each claim**

- **FACT**: seen on the source itself (official store page, official help centre, or the page the claim is about, including fan wikis that describe the game's own screens).
- **REPORTED**: a secondary source (analyst, journalist, blog, vendor newsletter, community answer). Their numbers are usually estimates from Sensor Tower or similar, not company data.
- **ESTIMATE / IDEA**: my own inference or proposal. Nothing in the idea sections is a fact about another game.

Store "What's New" dates on the US App Store pages show day and month without a year; I read them as 2026 because the pages were current on 2026-10-23 (ESTIMATE).

## 1. Game by game

### 1.1 Royal Match (Dream Games)

**King's Nightmare** (rescue puzzles taken from the game's video ads)

- Unlocks after a number of won levels; the player may skip it; a win pays **50 coins**; a loss **does not cost a life** and has no other penalty; several scenarios exist (kitchen fire, water tank, dragon, dynamite, parachute). FACT [S1]
- For a short while in 2022 it ran as a daily event. FACT [S1]
- Cadence: one appears every 100 levels, starting at level 50 (50, 150, 250 ...), slotted between the "-50" and "-51" levels; it does not count toward progression; sometimes a coin bonus level appears at that slot instead. REPORTED (blogger's own testing) [S3]
- The official Royal Match Facebook page says regular levels have no time limit but King's Nightmare levels give **60 seconds**, and another post title says each area hides one King's Nightmare. REPORTED (search-result snippet only; Facebook could not be opened) [S4]
- Launched February 2022. Purpose: turn the abstract puzzles shown in video ads into real level goals, avoiding "false marketing" and keeping CPI (cost per install) down. It first lived behind a daily button on the main screen with seven animations that signalled time pressure; metrics were weak, so Dream Games moved it "invisibly" into the level path between normal levels, where it reads as a palate cleanser. REPORTED [S2]
- Price: free; no purchase attached to it. FACT (no price on [S1]; not among the IAPs listed on [S5])

**Bonus levels (coin levels)**

- The store page promises players "loads of coins in bonus levels". FACT [S5]
- In 2021 a bonus level paid roughly 300–700 coins, against 900 coins for one standard extra-moves purchase; Deconstructor of Fun called this coin source rare among Western puzzle games. REPORTED [S6]

**Hard / Super Hard markers**

- Some levels are flagged "Hard" or "Super Hard"; even those can often be won with many moves left thanks to power-up combinations. REPORTED [S2]
- Several events pay **extra event currency on Hard and Super Hard levels**: extra pickaxes in Hidden Temple, extra Magic Balls in Dragon Nest, extra targets in Archery Arena. FACT [S7][S9][S10]

**Mini-game events fed by level wins** (all time-limited, rotating)

- *Hidden Temple*: 5 steps; winning levels gives pickaxes; the player digs a board to uncover gems; clearing a board opens the step's reward (boosters; 500 coins at step 3; 1000 coins plus boosters and card rewards at step 5). FACT [S7]
- *Dragon Nest*: co-op with one matched partner; raise 4 dragons, 3 stages each; Magic Balls come from levels and other events and are spent in a mini-game that earns Magic Tokens; grand prize includes a frame and 5000 coins; unused balls are lost when the event ends. FACT [S9]
- *Lava Quest*: beat 7 levels in a row on the first try within 24 hours; the player starts with 99 others; the 10,000-coin prize is split among all who finish. FACT [S8]
- *Archery Arena*: 50 players; targets from won levels; a multiplier grows with wins without failing and resets on a fail; rank-based rewards. FACT [S10]
- In early 2026 Naavik described Royal Match as running a "plethora of minigame events" to break the monotony of the core loop. REPORTED [S11]
- In 2025 Royal Match cut coin rewards from level wins by about 30% to make room for endlessly repeatable card collections. REPORTED [S12]

**Context**

- Current store events include an element-hunt event and a card collection; new areas with 100 new levels arrive in updates (latest seen: Lagoon, Sep 28). FACT [S5]
- Royal Pass is sold at $9.99. FACT [S5]
- Royal Match earned over $1.4B IAP revenue in 2025 (Sensor Tower). REPORTED [S11]

### 1.2 Royal Kingdom (Dream Games)

- **Kingdom levels**: two PvE "attack the Dark King" levels every ten levels; matches and boosters damage enemies and obstacles above the board, like a puzzle RPG. They are harder than normal hard levels, which makes them the main source of "out of moves" purchase moments. Royal Kingdom also gives more event tokens per 10 levels than Royal Match and fewer coins. REPORTED [S13]
- UK soft launch, first six months (Sensor Tower and Naavik): version 2 had ARPDAU $0.110 against Royal Match's $0.092 in its own soft launch, about 20% higher. REPORTED [S13]
- Meta: the player builds districts of a kingdom (Parliament Square, University, Princess Tower, latest: Cistern with 100 new levels); the store text frames everything as defending the kingdom from the Dark King. FACT [S14]
- Current events listed on the store page include **Spooky Carnival** ("find matching pairs to break the orbs", a pairs/memory mini-game), Richard's Journey, a card collection and Kingdom Pass ($9.99). FACT [S14]

### 1.3 Homescapes (Playrix)

**Mini Games** (Austin's bad dreams, pin puzzles)

- Introduced January 2020. Playable after a number of won levels; skippable; reward **50 coins and 1 star**; **24 hours** to beat one once offered. FACT [S15]
- Cadence today: every **5–10 levels from level 1 to 100**, then **every 50 levels**. FACT [S15]
- Types: pull out the pins/keys; use given tools to help Austin or save/clean a room; save family members or lead them to an exit; choose styles for Austin's childhood mansion. FACT [S15]
- September 2021: 10 mini-games beside 7,390 match-3 levels. FACT (Wikipedia, citing press) [S17]
- In April 2020 Playrix told the UK regulator there were about 10 such mini-games, usually **once every 20 levels**, and that only a very small share of players reached the far levels (reported as about 0.03%; the game then had 4,160 levels). Playrix also said about **45% of players reached level 20 and about 18% reached level 100**. REPORTED [S18]
- After the complaint Playrix said it moved this kind of puzzle toward the start of Homescapes and Gardenscapes so more players reach them. REPORTED [S18][S19]; Wikipedia states the game was changed this way. FACT [S17]

**Level types and markers**

| Type | Base coins | Base stars |
|---|---|---|
| Normal | 10 (was 50) | 1 |
| Hard | 100 | 1 |
| Super Hard | 150 | 1 |
| One Shot | 150 (was 300) | 3 |

FACT [S16]. Each type also has its own curtain colour and an Austin outfit, so the player sees the difference before playing. FACT [S16]

- "One Shot" levels are replays of earlier hard levels (in October 2022, hard levels from the range 1–54); apart from these and the Tournament of Champions, Homescapes levels cannot be replayed. REPORTED [S61]. Whether a One Shot level allows only one attempt is not verified.
- Homescapes has about 21,100 levels (September 2026); up to 50 new levels every Thursday or Friday. FACT [S16]

**Merge mini-game**: *Austin's Childhood*, a merge event inside Homescapes, later reused in Gardenscapes as *Secret Ingredient*. REPORTED [S24][S25]; listed among Homescapes events. FACT (wiki snippet) [S59]

### 1.4 Gardenscapes (Playrix)

- Mini-game mode *Austin's Bad Dreams*: appears now and then after a regular level, pays some coins; the guide notes most players tap "skip". REPORTED [S23]
- The developers said the ads showed mini-games available every 20 levels. REPORTED (Wikipedia summary of the controversy) [S22]
- After its pin-pull ads succeeded, Playrix put some of the advertised gameplay into Homescapes and Gardenscapes as mini-games. REPORTED [S21]
- **Secret Ingredient** (merge mini-game event): energy comes from beating base levels, from currency, or a free daily claim in an event shop; the player spends energy on generators, merges ingredients into dishes, dishes fill a reward meter; rewards are currency, energy, boosts for match-3 levels and profile pictures; the event shop sells ingredients and energy. The newsletter frames merge mini-games as a **retention** tool and pin-pull mini-games as a **user-acquisition** tool. REPORTED (vendor newsletter, not internal data) [S24]
- Liquid & Grit: Playrix reuses mini-games across several apps, which lets it build unusually robust mechanics, and uses them to target retention; the detailed analysis is behind a member login. REPORTED [S25]
- Store text today: expeditions, competitions, pets, story chapters; latest update a Halloween event. FACT [S26]
- History: Gardenscapes on mobile first soft-launched in 2015 as a **hidden-object** game with the same story and characters; it did not meet expectations; Playrix switched to match-3 late in development (2016). The analyst's explanation: hidden object has no real failure state and monetises mainly through hints. Pivot = REPORTED fact; reasoning = analyst opinion [S28]

### 1.5 Fishdom (Playrix)

- Minigames appear at random in place of a normal level, marked by a puzzle icon, after "a certain amount" of match-3 play; completing one pays diamonds and coins; a wrong move kills the fish and loses the minigame; replays are allowed. REPORTED [S29]
- The guide covers 10 minigames: grow-the-fish fights, rotate pipes or propellers to route water, pull pins, slide matching bars, choose a weapon against jellyfish. REPORTED [S29]
- Meta: unlock aquariums, decorate them, collect fish and pets. Current events include an expedition, a collection-card set and a **merge event** ("Romantic Venice": merge items, complete orders). FACT [S30]

### 1.6 Candy Crush Saga (King)

- **Dreamworld** (alternate mode, now removed): a parallel map unlocked at level 50; levels mirrored the main world but added a "Moon Scale" where the owl Odus had to be kept in balance by matching two colours evenly; filling the scale triggered "Moon Struck", which removed colours for a few moves; 665 levels; boosters for completing it; a Dreamworld level could not be played before its main-world twin. Removed from mobile on **11 December 2016**; King Care said it was dropped to focus on new levels in the normal world and on special events. FACT (wiki, quoting King Care) [S31]
- **Difficulty labels**: "Super Hard" levels were tested in December 2016 and released in February 2017 (mobile HTML5 in January 2017); they have their own map icon (a thunderstorm, later a skull) and colour scheme; the list changes roughly weekly; since 23 March 2022 any number of levels per episode can carry the label. FACT [S32]. King also marks "nightmarishly hard" and "legendary" levels. FACT (wiki snippet) [S33]
- **Timed levels were removed** (wiki dates the removal of extra-time candies to 2 May 2018). REPORTED (search summary of wiki pages) [S37]
- The store page advertises "unique game modes like Gummy Dragons and Bubblegum Pop". FACT [S35]. Fan wiki level pages show these as level objectives/blockers (drop gummy dragons, clear bubblegum layers) rather than separate modes. REPORTED [S35 note, wiki level pages via search]
- **Daily Booster Wheel**: one spin per day; needs an internet connection. FACT (wiki snippet) [S34]
- Weekly updates with new levels; time-limited themed events, tournaments, daily login rewards. FACT [S35]
- GDC 2024 talk by King (reported by mobilegamer.biz): level 65 was once the top converting and top revenue level but also the top churn point; making it easier kept players longer; fixing the 100 least fun levels gave "a very significant uplift in engagement"; harder levels should be shorter. REPORTED (first-party talk, no figures disclosed) [S36]

### 1.7 Toon Blast (Peak Games)

From the official help centre (all FACT):

- **Rescue Mission**: a mini-game where the player saves Bruno from dangers within a limited time by collecting items; a fail **does not cost lives**; the reward stays the same on retry; more mini-games come as the player progresses through main levels. [S39]
- **Surprise Calendar**: tap falling gifts for coins within a time limit, avoid TNT (it deducts coins); **once per day**; level 35+; the player must be **online**; 6 days of play give a surprise gift. [S40]
- **Cannon Fest**: time-limited, several stages; cannonballs come from winning levels; clear each stage's platform to claim its reward. [S41]
- **Space Miner**: time-limited stages; pickaxes come from completed levels, more if the level was won without a fail; dig tiles to find all hidden gems; a hidden robot clears several tiles. [S42]
- **Temple Guardians**: a merge-ball mini-game (two same-colour, same-size balls merge, four states, colour-specific bonuses) played together with partners; partners can be sent balls. [S43]
- **Renovation Event**: time-limited; earns boosters, unlimited lives and a **unique renovation area that stays in the album after the event ends**. [S44]
- Other events in the list: Dragon's Treasure (7 consecutive first-try wins), Crown Rush (start-of-level power-ups), Toon Pass, Star and Team Tournaments, Weekly Contest, Deep Quest. [S38]
- Episodes of 50 levels; a new episode every 2 weeks (latest seen: 12,401–12,450 on 12 Oct 2026). FACT [S45]
- A board element called **Puzzle Pieces**: hit twice to open; a completed puzzle disappears and reveals what is behind it. FACT [S46]
- The store text says the game is "packed with addictive mini-games and challenges". FACT [S47]

### 1.8 Lily's Garden (Tactile)

- Store text: story-led renovation of a garden; current events include a Halloween treasure hunt (collect seven keys) and a "Trick or Treat Pass"; weekly updates with new levels and story. FACT [S48]
- Seasonal events let the player redecorate a **separate themed area** through a special run of levels (the 2019 Christmas event had 57 levels); "Rocket Ruckus" (bank a power-up tally after each win; a loss resets it) and a win-streak reward (up to three starting power-ups). The story (with four writers) is the main differentiator. REPORTED [S49]
- Separate mini-games: **not confirmed** (one search result claimed them, but neither the US App Store text nor a store mirror mentions them).

### 1.9 Project Makeover (Magic Tavern)

- Store text: makeovers for clients, fashion-themed puzzles; current events include a "Timed Makeover" (60 levels) and a haunted "Journey"; past updates added season passes. FACT [S50]
- Three meta loops (make-up, dress-up, decoration) on top of a match-3 core that mainly generates soft currency; episodic, soap-opera story. REPORTED [S51]
- 2020 context: "meta match-3" grew +57% year over year against +5% for classic match-3. REPORTED [S51]
- Separate mini-games: **not found**.

### 1.10 June's Journey (Wooga) — hidden object with a jigsaw side game

- Core: hidden-object scenes (find listed items; faster finds score higher; wrong taps cost points); up to **5 stars** per scene; replaying a scene is how stars are earned. Each chapter = 5 hidden-object scenes + 1 **Adventure Scene** (more puzzle-like, no time bonus). Decorating Orchid Island earns Flowers that unlock scenes and chapters. FACT [S52][S53]
- Energy: a round costs at least 15 energy (10 for 10 minutes after watching an ad); a beginner's bar holds about 110; 1 energy every 2 minutes. REPORTED [S54]
- **Jigsaw side game**: character cards drop from star boxes (about 1 in 10 blue boxes, 1 in 3 pink boxes); 7 cards of one character unlock that character's **jigsaw puzzle**, which refills the energy bar (110, or 150 with the extended bar); puzzles can be saved in the photo album. REPORTED (community answer, 2019–2020) [S55]
- Scene variants such as "word mode" and "silhouette mode" exist according to community guides. REPORTED, weak (snippets only) [S56]

## 3. Variety inside the core loop

- **Visible difficulty markers with their own look** (map icon, colours, character outfit) and **larger rewards**: Candy Crush Super Hard / Nightmarishly Hard / Legendary [S32][S33]; Homescapes Hard 100 and Super Hard 150 coins vs 10 for normal [S16]; Royal Match Hard / Super Hard [S2]. FACT/REPORTED as cited.
- **Event currency multiplied on hard levels** turns a feared level into a wanted one (Royal Match [S7][S9][S10]). FACT
- **Replay formats**: Homescapes One Shot levels (150 coins, 3 stars) [S16], replays of earlier hard levels [S61]. FACT for rewards; replay rule REPORTED.
- **Boss framing**: Royal Kingdom's Dark King levels place the enemy above the board and make every match an attack [S13]. REPORTED
- **Timed boards**: Candy Crush removed timed levels in 2018 [S37] (REPORTED), while Royal Match uses a 60-second timer only in the optional, no-penalty King's Nightmare [S1][S4]. ESTIMATE: time pressure is tolerated when it is optional and free to fail.
- **New board elements on a steady schedule**: Toon Blast introduces elements at fixed level numbers (crystals at 12,101, bulb factory at 10,501 ...) [S46]; Royal Match's latest update added 100 levels together with a new obstacle (Hologram) [S5]. FACT
- **Level quality pruning** rather than more content: King finds and fixes the least fun levels; longer levels are less likely to be fun [S36]. REPORTED
- **Short episodes with a name and theme** (Toon Blast 50-level episodes every 2 weeks [S45]; Royal Match areas of 100 levels [S5]). FACT

## 4. What the evidence says

| # | Finding | Numbers | Source | Strength and caveats |

| E9 | Very hard levels convert but churn; fun levels retain | Easing level 65 kept players longer though it cut conversion; fixing the 100 least fun levels gave a "very significant uplift in engagement" | [S36] | **Medium-strong**: first-party A/B tests presented at GDC, but no numbers disclosed |

| E11 | Players stall when lives run out and there is nothing else to do | Royal Match 2021 review; recommendation: side progression and more events | [S6] | **Weak** (expert opinion), but it is the problem no-life-cost mini-games solve |
| E12 | Every new reward source needs an economy counterweight | Royal Match cut level coin rewards about 30% when adding endless collections | [S12] | **Medium-weak** (analyst observation) |
| E13 | Grinding the core to feed a meta annoys players | Reviews complain about how often they must play to farm currency | [S58] | **Weak** (2020 opinion piece) |

| E16 | Daily/streak "jackpot" events are now near universal | Lava Quest-type events in almost every top casual puzzle title; D90/D1 relative retention converging near 25% (iOS US) | [S12] | **Medium-weak** (analyst; retention not tied to the feature) |

**Bottom line (ESTIMATE):** no public source isolates a large retention gain from mini-games. The best numbers (E1, E3) suggest a small revenue effect and a possible small retention effect, while economy features (pass, prize roads, competitions) carry revenue. The consistent behaviour of every leader (E4, E5, E7) — short, optional, free-to-fail breaks placed inline, front-loaded in the first 100 levels, plus rotating mini-boards fed by level wins — is itself the strongest signal that they keep paying for themselves through retention. For this game the most solid lessons are E6/E7 (put variety early), E9 (do not buy variety with extreme difficulty) and E12 (run every new reward through the economy simulator).

## 5. Ideas that fit a museum and painting-restoration theme

**These are ideas (ESTIMATE / IDEA), not facts.** Coin and reward amounts are deliberately left out: the project's rule is that the economy simulator (GAME_DESIGN §25) decides them. Cadences assume the current structure: 1 painting = 10 levels (saw-tooth, the 10th is the hardest), 1 room = 50 levels, 20 rooms.

| ID | Idea | How it plays | Placement and cadence | Reward | Life cost | Inspired by |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| I1 | **Restorer's Bench** (interstitial puzzles) | A one-screen puzzle using the painting just completed. Notes on the ideas (ESTIMATE):

- **Cost order**: I5 < I3 < I4 < I12 < I2 < I1c/I1b/I1a < I13 < I9 < I7 < I6 < I8 < I10 < I11. I1, I3, I4 and I5 reuse paintings and the engine the game already has.
- **Licences for I1b/I1c/I8**: altering an image (forgery copy) or adding new paintings must stay within each museum's terms; CC0 releases allow modification, but each source in `art/paintings/room-NN/licences.json` should be checked before an altered copy ships.
- **Do not let I2/I4 become extreme**: E9 says extreme spikes cost players; the boss frame should change the look, not the difficulty curve that the bots calibrated.
- **Avoid Dreamworld** (E10): no parallel 1000-level map; keep variety as short breaks and events around the main path.
- **Placement**: E6 and E7 argue for putting I1 densely in levels 1–100 and thinning it later.
- **Ethics already in the design** (§17, §19): no paid random packs; I9 should not use simulated opponents presented as real people.

- Homescapes "One Shot" levels: rewards and the replay rule are sourced; whether only one attempt is allowed is not verified.
- Lily's Garden and Project Makeover separate mini-games: not confirmed.
- Candy Crush "game modes" (Gummy Dragons, Bubblegum Pop): store calls them modes, wiki pages show level objectives; no official description found.
- June's Journey word/silhouette modes: community snippets only.
- Liquid & Grit's Secret Ingredient analysis is behind a member login.
- No GDC talk specifically about mini-games was found; Sensor Tower / AppMagic / data.ai public posts on mini-games specifically were not found.
- Store "What's New" dates show no year.
- Painting-themed match-3 titles (Match-3 Painter, Epic Paint Adventure, Art & Puzzles) seen only in search snippets; TouchArcade returned 403.
