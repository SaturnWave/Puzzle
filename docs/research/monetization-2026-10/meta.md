# The meta layer around match-3 levels: what the top games do, what the evidence says, and ideas for the museum

Research for the museum-restoration match-3 game. Written 2026-10-23.

**Labels.** Every claim carries a label and a source number ([S#]). The source list at the end gives the URL, the publication date where known, and the date I saw it (2026-10-23 for every source).

- **FACT**: I saw it myself on the source: an official page, a store listing or the game's own help.
- **REPORTED**: it comes from a secondary source, such as an analyst, a journalist, a fan wiki or a fan guide. Fan wikis describe the game well but are not official, so they count as REPORTED.
- **ESTIMATE**: my own inference or arithmetic.

I quote nothing at length. Everything is in my own words.

## 1. What each game does

### 1.1 Royal Match (Dream Games): castle areas paid with stars

**How it works**
- King Robert's castle is split into areas that unlock one after another. Each area has its own tasks. Completing all of an area's tasks gives an Area Chest (coins, boosters and cards), and then the next area opens. FACT [S1]; REPORTED [S2][S4]
- Stars are the only currency for tasks. Each level beaten gives 1 star, and stars cannot be bought. REPORTED, fan wiki [S2]. The official help says only that stars come from beating levels. FACT [S1]
- **Example area, Museum (area 15):** 9 tasks with star costs of 4, 6, 7, 4, 7, 6, 8, 4 and 4, so the area costs 50 stars. Some tasks wait for earlier ones. The tasks include hanging paintings, hanging a portrait, placing a bust and naming the artworks. REPORTED [S3]. At 1 star per win, that area takes about 50 won levels, which is the same as one room in our game (50 levels). ESTIMATE
- Royal Match also has areas called "Art Studio", "Antique Room" and "Fossil Museum". The wiki lists 111 areas, and that list may be out of date. REPORTED [S4]
- Content cadence: the store's What's New note (version 38911, 28 Sep) lists 100 new levels, a new area (Lagoon) and new events. The same note says new levels arrive every two weeks. FACT [S9]

**Character and story**
- The story has no text. King Robert's face at the top of the screen reacts to the player's progress, and there is no decoration choice. Naavik describes this as removing the narrative and customisation that Homescapes and Lily's Garden have. REPORTED [S11]
- **King's Nightmare:** optional "rescue the king" levels (a kitchen fire, a water tank, a dragon). They can be skipped. Winning one gives 50 coins, and failing costs no life. REPORTED [S5]. When the feature launched in February 2022, revenue rose 1%, downloads fell 21%, and D14 retention rose almost 3% month over month. REPORTED [S11]

**Meta tests**
- Naavik says it observed an A/B/C test of the meta in the game's first year:
  - A: no meta at all;
  - B: the background changes at set levels, with no reward;
  - C: episode backgrounds bought with stars, plus rewards.

  The control group had today's areas and tasks. Some players are reportedly still held in old variants. Naavik had no access to Dream's data and published no results. It also says Dream runs about 60 A/B tests a year, a figure from a 2023 university talk. REPORTED [S12]
- A 2025 Naavik piece describes the meta as a long-term goal that never overshadows the core puzzle. REPORTED [S13]

**Card collection ("Royal Collection")**
- The event lasts 62 days. It has 15 sets of 9 cards (135 cards), and cards are rated 1 to 5 stars. A few gold cards cannot be gifted. REPORTED [S7]
- Packs come as rewards from events and the Royal Pass. They come in five colours:

  | Pack | Contents |
  |---|---|
  | Brown | 2 cards |
  | Green | 3 cards |
  | Blue | 4 cards |
  | Purple | 6 cards |
  | Red | at least 1 new card |

  REPORTED [S7]
- **Duplicates** add their star value to a "card stars" balance. That balance buys chests at 100, 250 or 500 stars, which contain packs and boosters. Duplicates can also be sent to friends. FACT [S6] for the mechanism; REPORTED [S7] for the tiers and their contents
- Gifting is limited to 3 cards a day, only to friends. REPORTED [S7]
- **Album reward:** 10,000 coins, boosters and a profile badge. A second "Grand Royal Collection" then resets the album and asks for the same cards again. REPORTED [S7]
- **Are packs sold?**
  - The US App Store's list of top in-app purchases shows no card pack. That list includes the Royal Pass at $9.99. FACT [S9]
  - A fan guide says red packs have appeared in paid offers from time to time, and that the premium Royal Pass includes one red, five blue and four purple packs. REPORTED [S8]
  - An App Store story about a seasonal pass lists card packs among the pass rewards. FACT, seen in a search snippet [S10]
  - So random cards reach paying players through the pass and offers. **Royal Match does not avoid paid random items.** ESTIMATE from [S8][S9][S10]
- Naavik's 2022 view: the collection was the game's first permanent secondary progression, but probably would not move long-term retention much. REPORTED [S11]

**Royal Pass (as a meta currency)**
- Players beat levels to collect keys, and keys open the pass rewards. A seasonal pass listing describes 30 stages and a premium track with extra lives (8 instead of 5) and a coin bank. FACT, seen in store snippets [S10]
- When the Royal Pass launched in June 2021, revenue rose 31% over the first 30 days and D30 retention rose 2%. REPORTED [S11]

**Scale**
- AppMagic: $5 billion gross player spending and $3.1 billion net IAP from 325 million downloads (January 2025). By May 2025 gross spending had passed $6 billion, or $3.6 billion net. REPORTED [S36][S37]
- Naavik, citing Sensor Tower: more than $4 billion lifetime and about 55 million monthly users (August 2025). REPORTED [S13]

### 1.2 Royal Kingdom (Dream Games): kingdom districts, an enemy king and characters

- The player builds a kingdom district by district (the store names Parliament Square, the University and the Princess Tower). The cast is King Richard, who is King Robert's younger brother, Princess Bella and a Wizard. Special levels defend the kingdom from the Dark King's castles and minions. FACT [S15]
- **Meta currency: potions.** Potions are earned by beating levels and spent on restoration tasks. REPORTED [S14]
- **Version 2, after a hard reset a year into soft launch** REPORTED [S14]:
  - a bird's-eye view focused on finishing whole districts;
  - themed extra kingdoms (Ice, Jungle) after the main one, to hold players in the mid and late game;
  - the economy moved to the side of the screen;
  - the earlier PvP boss levels, in which friends or strangers could attack your castle, replaced by a PvE Dark King. Deconstructor of Fun reads this as less social play but a stronger story.
- **Soft-launch numbers:** UK ARPDAU about $0.14, compared with $0.25 or more for peers. Peak month December 2023, at about $1.0 million IAP. REPORTED [S14]
- The store lists a Kingdom Pass at $9.99. FACT [S15]
- AppMagic: $29.2 million net IAP by January 2025. REPORTED [S36]

### 1.3 Homescapes and Gardenscapes (Playrix): story days, a recurring butler and style choices

**What the store pages say**
- Homescapes: beating levels unlocks story chapters, and the player decides how the house looks. Characters interact through an in-game social network, and the player can invite Facebook friends. FACT [S16]
- Homescapes' latest note (version 9.2.102, 14 Oct) adds a "Night in the Castle" event and a story chapter, "Two Halves", led by the characters Patrick and Lisa. So the story now carries characters beyond Austin. FACT [S16]
- Gardenscapes: Austin the butler guides a storyline across garden areas. The store lists more than 16,000 levels, pets, friends, Expeditions and competitions. FACT [S18]

**Task structure, from the fan wiki's page on the Homescapes Hall** REPORTED [S17]
- The room is split into story "days".
- Every task costs 1 star, and the Hall takes 57 stars in all.
- Each day pays a reward at 50% and another at 100%.
- Story beats and decoration are mixed in one list: "Name the cat", "Give dad a present" and "Meet Katherine" sit beside "Install a bookcase".
- Some tasks are style choices ("Choose banisters"). A design can be changed later by tapping and holding the item.
- Houses are grouped into large locations (Family Mansion, Lake House, Mountain Estate, Island Hotel, Downtown). The wiki's menu lists one-time events called Collections, Expeditions and Home Pass.

**Character and studio**
- Playrix's business head says Austin's looks were drawn from 1960s-70s icons. He says Austin's personality matters more than his looks: Austin has opinions, makes mistakes and jokes. Austin has his own Instagram account. These are Playrix's own claims, not measured data. REPORTED [S19]
- In the same interview, Gardenscapes started as a PC hidden-object game and moved to match-3 around 2014. The mobile game also includes mini-games (mini puzzles, collapse and merge). REPORTED [S19]
- Playrix had more than 4,000 employees at that time, which shows the content budget behind a heavy meta. REPORTED [S19]

**Scale**
- AppMagic (January 2025): Gardenscapes $4.0 billion net IAP from 672 million downloads; Homescapes $3.4 billion net from 619 million. REPORTED [S36]
- Sensor Tower (data to Q4 2019): revenue per install of $7.07 for Gardenscapes and $4.52 for Homescapes. Sensor Tower credits Austin as an iconic protagonist but does not measure his effect. REPORTED [S20]

### 1.4 Matchington Mansion (Magic Tavern): rooms, DIY decoration and comparing with neighbours

- The game has dozens of rooms and thousands of DIY decoration options. The story pits the player against a mischievous cousin, with help from a friend named Tiffany. The player can keep a cat or a dog, and can look at neighbours' homes to compare design choices. FACT [S21]
- The latest note (version 1.212.0, 16 Oct) adds a story chapter, a new room ("Magical Academy P1") and an event. FACT [S21]. Content arrives as room plus story chapter plus event.
- The store page does not explain the stars-to-tasks loop. A user review mentions stars paying for tasks. FACT that the review says so [S21]; the mechanism itself is not verified.
- AppMagic: $716 million net IAP from 203 million downloads (January 2025). REPORTED [S36]

### 1.5 Lily's Garden (Tactile Games): story chosen in small pieces

- Each level won gives 1 star. Small chores cost 1 star and bigger ones 2 or 3. Each finished task unlocks a short story passage. All tasks of a "day" must be done before the next day opens. REPORTED [S23]
- When two or three tasks are open, the player chooses which story thread to read next, and can save stars to read several in a row. The choices change only the order and pace of the story; nothing branches. REPORTED [S23][S24]
- Emily Short calls this "storylet" design and thinks it suits mobile free-to-play well. She also notes that decoration choices (flowers, benches) are visible but largely separate from the story. REPORTED [S24]
- The story deals with grown-up themes such as divorce, custody and death. GameRefinery reports that four writers were on the payroll, a team of about 50, no ads, and a top-50 grossing rank in Q4 2019. Seasonal events give a separate area to redecorate; the Christmas event had 57 levels. REPORTED [S25]
- The store page offers hundreds of flowers and dozens of customisation options. The latest notes (versions 3.85.0, dated 15 Oct, and 3.86.0) promise "the next part of Lily's story". FACT [S22]

### 1.6 Project Makeover (Magic Tavern): a new client every episode

**Structure, from the fan wiki** REPORTED [S27]
- Each makeover is one client with a short backstory. The villain Greta tries to talk the client out of it.
- There are three stages: hair (with Francis), wardrobe (with Gigi) and room (with Derek). Then comes "The Reveal", a before-and-after montage.
- Afterwards the client sends a **thank-you letter** with a gem gift.
- Seasons hold 5 or 6 makeovers, and the wiki lists 27 seasons. One client is a painter and another an artist.

**Store page and analysis**
- The player chooses clothes, hairstyles, makeup and furniture, dresses their own avatar, and can visit friends' avatars. FACT [S26]
- The latest note (version 2.149.1, 12 Oct) adds a 60-level timed makeover challenge. FACT [S26]
- GameRefinery's guest analysis (February 2021) describes three meta loops: makeup, dress-up and decoration. It quotes the CEO as treating match-3 mainly as a way to earn soft currency. REPORTED [S28]
- AppMagic: $755 million net IAP from 275 million downloads (January 2025). REPORTED [S36]

### 1.7 Collection metas (albums, stickers, pins)

**Monopoly GO (Scopely)**
This is a board game, not match-3, but it is the largest album meta in casual games.
- **Rarity:** stickers come in 1 to 5 stars, plus gold stickers. Wild stickers become any missing sticker. FACT [S38]
- **Where packs come from:** events, tournaments, daily quick wins, the colour wheel, Community Chest mini-games and **shop bundles**. FACT [S38]
- **Duplicates** turn into stars. Stars open vaults costing 250, 300 or 1,500 stars, which give dice, packs and wild stickers. FACT [S38]. An earlier album used 250, 500 and 800, so the costs change each season. REPORTED [S39]
- **Trading:** up to five duplicates can go to friends. Gold stickers can be traded only during "Golden Blitz" events. FACT [S38]
- **Album end:** unfinished stickers vanish when an album ends. REPORTED [S39] Leftover stars are converted. One season gave at most 750 rolls for 700 or more stars. FACT, an official post seen in a search excerpt [S47]
- **Album size:** albums run about 7 to 8 weeks, with 16 to 22 sets of 9 stickers. One 2025 album was art-themed ("Artful Tales"). REPORTED [S45]
- **Store label:** Google Play labels the game "In-Game Purchases (Includes Random Items)". FACT [S38]
- **Scale:** Sensor Tower estimates more than $6 billion lifetime IAP. Scopely says the game still earns about $200 million a month. Sensor Tower credits events, friend interactions, gifting and trading. REPORTED [S40]. The 1,275 days to reach $6 billion is REPORTED [S41]
- Real-money sticker trading has grown up on outside marketplaces. REPORTED [S41]

**Candy Crush Saga (King): "Pins"**
- 15 seasonal collection sets. Pins are earned **only through gameplay** (base features and events) and are **not sold**. REPORTED [S42]
- Liquid & Grit (May 2025): 24 of the 60 casual apps it tracks run a seasonal collection, and collections were the most-released season type over the previous year. REPORTED [S42]

- Google Play's Payments policy has the same odds-disclosure rule. Google's country page adds that Brazil bans loot boxes in games aimed at or likely to be used by children and adolescents. REPORTED, from a Google search summary that I did not read in full [S44]
- This game already bans paid random packs, which avoids both issues. ESTIMATE

- Every game turns one level win into a **small, visible change**. Royal Match shows one every 4 to 8 wins, and Homescapes and Lily's Garden every win.
- They also give a **bigger reveal at the end of a unit**: Royal Match's Area Chest, the day rewards in Homescapes, the Reveal in Project Makeover.
- This game already gives a per-win change (new pieces) and a reveal every 10 levels (a finished painting).
- What it lacks between paintings is a **room-level task list** that the player spends on. That list is what lets players choose an order, see the room change and plan ahead.

- **Strong:** controlled or large-sample data from a named data company.
- **Medium:** large-sample market data, but not controlled for other causes.
- **Weak:** a single case, the company's own claim, or opinion.

**Reading** (ESTIMATE, built from the rows above)

1. A meta is now the norm among top-grossing match-3 games (E1, E2, E11), but there is **no public controlled study** that isolates its effect on retention or spending.
2. Within that norm, **the heavy story-and-decoration meta does not beat the light one** on the public numbers. Royal Match leads in D30 (E4) and in revenue per download (E7), and Dream Games appears to have tested its way to a light meta (E6).
3. What does correlate with retention in the public data is **events and foreshadowed content** (E4), plus passes (E8).
4. Collections work at very large scale when they are social and tradable (E10). That needs friends, and therefore a server. As a single-player progression layer, analysts expected less (E9).
5. Heavy metas cost a lot to make: Playrix had more than 4,000 staff (S19) and Lily's Garden had 4 writers (S25). For a two-person team with no artist, a **light meta plus cheap, text-driven depth** is the realistic shape.
6. The weak spots of this evidence are the same throughout: it is mostly old (2019-2022), observational, and mixed with UA spending and launch timing.

## 4. Ideas that fit a museum and painting-restoration theme

**These are ideas, not facts.** Each one names the pattern it borrows from (with its source) and its cost for this team. Current decisions to respect:

- no paid random packs;
- the painter avatar is drawn by the owner, and agents do not draw people;
- the team is the owner plus Claude;
- 1 painting = 10 levels and 1 room = 50 levels;
- the side collection is bought with coins earned in play;
- the 2026-10-02 decision of a light museum meta with "no player choices".

| ID | Idea | Borrowed from | Content cost (ESTIMATE) |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | **Curator's task list per room.** Each win also gives one "brush" (or star). The room shows 8 to 12 restoration tasks costing 1 to 6 brushes, sized so that a room is about 50 wins: clean the varnish, fix the frame, relight the hall, write the plaque, open the doors. The room picture changes visibly with each task, so something happens between two finished paintings. | Royal Match areas and the Museum area [S1][S3]; Homescapes days [S17] | Layered room states (before/after sprites per task); no people |
| M2 | **Painting story card.** When a painting is finished, show a short plaque story: artist, year, place, the museum it comes from, and one plain fact. Link it to the existing world-map pin. Unlock a "read more" card per room. | Lily's Garden storylets [S23][S24]; the Homescapes letters in [S17] | Text only, but needs translation into all shipped languages |
| M3 | **Show what is coming.** Covered frames, scaffolding and silhouettes of the next room's paintings, plus a countdown ("3 paintings until the Edo Japan room opens"). | Foreshadowed content tied to high D30 [S34] | Reuses existing images, darkened or blurred |
| M4 | **Restoration touch on each finished painting.** A short swipe that removes grime or varnish from the completed jigsaw (dirty to clean), as a tactile reveal. | Project Makeover's "Reveal" [S27] | A dirt overlay per painting, which can be generated |
| M5 | **Room decoration choices.** For a few slots per room (frame style, wall colour, bench, lamp), choose 1 of 3, and allow changing it later. **This changes the 2026-10-02 "no player choices" decision and needs the owner's yes.** | Homescapes "choose banisters" and tap-and-hold to change [S17]; Matchington [S21] | Three art variants per slot, which is the most costly item here |
| M6 | **Curator rank (player level with XP).** XP from wins, moves left and boosters not used. Ranks run from Volunteer to Chief Curator and give small rewards. This answers the owner's request for a player level. | Pass and XP patterns [S10][S11] | Rank names and badges |
| M7 | **Museum catalogue album.** Each room is a "set" and each painting a "card". Set rewards: boosters at 5/5, a plaque at a full room. The coin-bought side collection is a separate wing of the same album. | Royal Match collection [S7]; Candy Crush pins, gameplay only [S42] | Mostly UI over data the game already has |

| M11 | **Detail hunt.** After a painting is finished, find three small details in it (a dog, a ship, a signature) for a few coins. This is a light hidden-object game on art the game already has. | Gardenscapes' hidden-object roots [S19] | Coordinates per painting |

Small notes on the ideas (ESTIMATE):
- **M1 + M2 + M3** together give a light meta close to Royal Match's tested shape [S12], with the "something every few wins" rhythm and foreshadowing [S34]. They add almost no art beyond room states.
- **M5** is the only idea that reverses an earlier decision.
- **M14** is the only idea that needs a backend.

## 5. Needs a server or not

The game currently has no server and no INTERNET permission.

**Offline on one phone** means the idea works with local save data and the device clock.

**Needs network, no own server** means the idea needs only a platform service: for example, content delivered by an app-store update (the store downloads it, not the app) or a platform save service. Whether a platform save service fits the INTERNET-permission constraint is my estimate, to be verified.

**Needs a backend** means the idea needs a server of our own (accounts, shared state, anti-cheat).

## Gaps and limits

- **No controlled public study** isolates the meta's effect on retention or spending. All of section 3 is observational, and much of it is 2019-2022.
- **Dream Games' meta test results** are not public. Naavik's account is from observation [S12].
- **Not verified:**
  - whether Homescapes, Lily's Garden or Royal Kingdom sell their meta currency directly;
  - how Project Makeover's tasks are paid for;
  - current Homescapes and Gardenscapes card-collection rules (no official Playrix help page found).
- **Monopoly GO's official help** on album rules was not found. Album sizes and lengths come from fan guides [S45]. Vault costs differ between seasons [S38][S39].
- **Google Play's loot-box policy text and the Brazil rule** were read only as a search summary [S44]. Apple's rule was read on Apple's page [S43].
- **Pages that refused or failed:**
  - medium.com (403);
  - royalmatch.fandom.com through plain fetch (402). I read it through Firecrawl instead.
  - Game Industry Library's GameRefinery report (the text was not served);
  - the InvestGame PDF of the same report (too large). I used the WN Hub summary of that report instead [S33].
- **Revenue figures** are third-party estimates (AppMagic, Sensor Tower) and disagree with each other. Revenue per download in E7 is my arithmetic.
